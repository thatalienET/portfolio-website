This is a self-initiated motion design exploration based on the existing brand identity created by Wild People Studio.


Having followed the identity for some time, I wanted to explore how its visual language could translate into motion, using the project as an opportunity to experiment with movement, composition, and the relationship between static brand elements and animation.


With no client brief or predefined deliverables, the project gave me the freedom to develop my own creative directon while strengthening my approach to brand-led motion design. It was also an opportunity to explore Cavalry more deeply and experiment With techniques I could carry into future identity and motion projects


Brand Identity: Wild People Studio
Motion Design: Me

This project was primarily an opportunity to challenge myself as a motion designer.


Rather than starting with a client brief or a defined set of requirements, I set myself the challenge of taking an existing brand identity and finding a way to extend its visual language through motion without compromising what made the identity work in the first place.


I wanted to push myself to think beyond simply animating individual elements and instead consider how movement could become part of the brand expression itself — exploring rhythm, timing, transitions and composition to create something that felt intentional and connected to the original identity.


I also used the project as an opportunity to step further outside my usual workflow and get more comfortable working in Cavalry. With no client constraints or expectations, I could experiment, make mistakes and iterate freely, using the project as a space to develop new techniques and broaden my motion design toolkit.

I approached the project by looking closely at the existing identity and identifying the visual characteristics that could naturally translate into motion. Rather than introducing a completely new visual language, I wanted the animation to feel like an extension of the brand itself.


I explored how the identity's shapes, typography and graphic elements could move, interact and transition between one another. This became less about adding movement for the sake of it and more about finding a consistent motion language that felt playful, considered and true to the original design.


Through experimentation and iteration in Cavalry, I developed a series of transitions and behaviours that allowed the individual brand elements to work together as a cohesive sequence. The result was a motion concept that builds on the existing identity while giving it a new dimension through movement.
